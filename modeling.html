Why a Decision Tree and a Random Forest

Decision Tree

  • Readable. The result is a set of yes / no rules you can follow by hand.
  • Fits our data. Works with a mix of categories and numbers; no scaling needed.
  • Weakness. One tree can memorise the training days.

Random Forest

  • Steadier. Averages 100 different trees, so one odd split matters less.
  • Finds combinations. For example "weekend and holiday and this line".
  • Weakness. Harder to read, so we show feature importance instead.
